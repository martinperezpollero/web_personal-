I think of play and leisure as fundamental dimensions of human experience.
How, where, and with whom we play expresses a good part of who we are, and
of the inequalities that run through our lives.

I'm a technician in Leisure-Time Education and Recreation from the
Universidad Católica del Uruguay, a sociologist, and I hold a master's in
Information and Communication from the Universidad de la República, along
with a postgraduate diploma in Social and Political Anthropology from
FLACSO Argentina. I'm currently doing my PhD in Leisure, Culture and
Communication for Human Development at the University of Deusto (Bilbao).
I'm on the faculty of the Universidad Católica del Uruguay, where I teach in
the BA in Educational Recreation and mentor students training to use play
as an educational and social tool.

I like to think about how I work through the figure of the bricoleur, as
Lévi-Strauss describes it: someone who doesn't start from tools designed
for a single purpose, but works with whatever is at hand, recombining
pieces from different origins to build something new. My path through
recreation, sociology, communication, and anthropology works the same way
— a mixed toolkit I draw on for each problem. That same logic runs through
my research, where I combine life-story accounts, ethnographic fieldwork,
and structural analysis to understand play in its singularity and in its
social conditions. And that's not a coincidence: playing, too, is often a
form of bricolage.

My work sits at the crossroads of Leisure Studies and Play Studies. I'm
especially interested in the biographical dimension of play experiences:
what we play throughout our lives, what we were and weren't able to play,
and how that relates to social class, territory, and institutions. From
there I work on topics like play inequality, the right to play, educational
leisure in settings of confinement, public recreational spaces, and the
political socialization of young people.

I've published in academic journals and books across Latin America and
Europe, and I combine research with teaching and work alongside educational
and community organizations.

I live and work in Montevideo. If you'd like to talk about play, leisure,
recreation, or research, get in touch.
